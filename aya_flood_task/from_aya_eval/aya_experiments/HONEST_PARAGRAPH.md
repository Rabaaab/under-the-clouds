# Honest paragraph

On the part of the Ksar El Kebir scene covered by the Zenodo reference (634,522 pixels), the threshold baseline scored IoU 0.442 and F1 0.613, and the U-Net scored IoU 0.256 and F1 0.407. Precision was similar (0.480 vs 0.502), but the U-Net found only 34% of the reference water, against 85% for the threshold. We do not claim the U-Net is better here. It behaves like a stricter dark-pixel rule: the water it shares with the reference has a median of -23.6 dB, while the water it missed has a median of -19.5 dB. Its extra detections are also very dark (median -22.6 dB), so some may be water the reference does not label; this is not confirmed.

These numbers are limited. The reference masks are semi-automatic (HSBA and Otsu thresholding plus visual checks), not expert ground truth, and 85% of their water pixels lie below the scene's Otsu threshold, so they probably favour the baseline. They are water masks, not a flood-change map, they cover 26.33% of the scene, and their dates could not be verified against the 8 February 2026 scene. The U-Net's benchmark results (IoU 0.687 on the Sen1Floods11 test split, 0.601 on Bolivia) did not carry over to this reference.

Failure cases: in the Otsu crops, most false alarms are large dark areas that look like flooded or smooth fields, which the reference does not label, so part of the disagreement may be a reference limit. Most missed water is patches with only a weak darkening in VV, which a single threshold cannot catch. None of the five Otsu crops clearly shows steep terrain, a city or the sea.
